EXPLORE THE SCIENCE

Discover the World of Cyclones

Cyclones are powerful natural disasters that can affect thousands of people, especially those living in coastal areas. Understanding how they form, the risks they bring and how early warnings work can help communities prepare before a storm arrives.

01 / THE BASICS

What Is a Cyclone?

A cyclone is a large rotating storm that develops over warm ocean water. It forms around an area of low pressure and can bring powerful winds, heavy rainfall and thunderstorms. Cyclones are also known as hurricanes or typhoons in different parts of the world.

02 / FORMATION

How Do They Form?

Cyclones begin when warm ocean water heats the air above it. The warm, moist air rises, creating low pressure. More air moves in and begins to rotate. Under suitable conditions, the storm grows stronger and develops into a cyclone.

03 / THE RISKS

Know the Dangers

Cyclones can cause serious damage through strong winds, heavy rainfall, flooding and storm surges. These hazards can destroy homes, damage roads and disrupt electricity and communication systems, making coastal communities especially vulnerable.

UNDERSTAND THE THREATS

Four Key Cyclone Hazards

Learn about the major dangers associated with cyclones.

💨

Strong Winds

Powerful winds can damage buildings, break trees and create dangerous flying debris.

🌧️

Heavy Rainfall

Continuous rainfall can cause floods, landslides and unsafe road conditions.

🌊

Storm Surges

Rising seawater can move inland and flood low-lying coastal communities.

📢

Early Warnings

Weather alerts help people prepare, evacuate and move to safer locations in time.

KNOWLEDGE SAVES LIVES

Why Is Cyclone Awareness Important?

Cyclone Nargis in 2008 showed how devastating a cyclone can be for Myanmar. Many coastal communities continue to face risks from strong storms and flooding. By learning about cyclone warnings and disaster preparedness, younger generations can become more informed and help their families and communities stay safer.
